# Tone

Who the game is for, and the undertone under everything we write and draw. Matt set it on [GitHub issue #22](https://github.com/RktRobinhood/wasteland-academy/issues/22) (2026-10-24, FAL-350). Read it before you write a line, a quest, an encounter, a class name or a character's look.

`SPEC.md` still sets the world (Salvage Neon Bazaar), and `writing/voice-sheets.md` still sets each voice. This file sits on top of both. Studio Manager checks new writing against it.

## Who it's for

IB Maths AA SL students: high school, 16 to 19, often playing in class. They live inside IB culture: the workload, the deadlines, the jokes on r/IBO and the rest of the internet. Write for them, not for their teachers. A reference only an IB student would catch is a good one.

## The undertone: IB survivors

"IB survivor" is one of the biggest IB memes. Our quiet joke is that the wasteland is full of them. They got through high school, and now they use what it taught them to survive out here. The wasteland is the unknown that comes after school: university, work, whatever's next.

Nobody in the game says so. It's subtext. Students should half-notice it and grin.

- **Hint, never name.** No line says "IB", "International Baccalaureate" or "Diploma Programme", and nobody explains the joke. This also keeps a later paid release clear of the IB's trademarks.
- **A light touch.** Most lines carry no reference at all. One wink in an encounter or a scene is plenty, and plenty have none. In Matt's words, the memes "aren't a big part of our game, those are just the subtext".
- **Survivors are tough, not broken.** Getting through made these people capable. The joke is fond and shared. It's never at the expense of a student who finds the maths hard.
- **What comes next is unknown, not grim.** Hints about life after school (an offer letter kept like a relic, a far-off city everyone calls Uni, the road out) stay curious and hopeful. Never joke that a bad grade ruins your life. The students playing this worry about exactly that.

### Things to draw on

These are examples, not a checklist. Turn them into wasteland things (trades, rituals, sayings, place names) rather than quoting them straight.

- **Relics.** A formula booklet kept like scripture. A calculator carried like a sidearm. A faded T-shirt that says "I SURVIVED", with the rest torn off.
- **The words.** Command terms like "show that" and "hence or otherwise". Mark schemes. Paper 1 and Paper 2. Forty-five, the perfect score, which most people treat as a legend. Out here they become sayings, oaths and the way a toll keeper talks.
- **The rest of the diploma.** The 4,000-word Extended Essay, CAS, TOK's "but how do we know?", internal assessments, predicted grades. A hermit still writing the same 4,000 words. A gate that wants proof of service. A philosopher who answers everything with "but how do you know?"
- **May.** The exam season. Old-timers can talk about May the way sailors talk about storms.
- **Sleep and caffeine.** Nobody out here has slept properly in years. The coffee cart already fits.

## High school tropes

High school is the texture under the setting. The jock, the cheerleader, the class clown, the mathlete, the drama kid, the student-council type and the kid who's always late can all shape characters, subclasses and looks. Matt's example: one of Spark's subclasses could be the cheerleader and another the jock.

- **Play them warm.** The jock is great at something. The cheerleader keeps the team going. Nobody is the butt of a joke about looks, body, gender or brains.
- **Tropes go on subclasses and characters, never on the player.** Any player can pick any subclass, so the cheerleader branch isn't a girls' branch. Lines still never gender the player.
- **No strong gender tropes.** Matt, on FAL-345: "the only one I want you to avoid is strong gender tropes." Tilly's and Banjo's tropes are ones nobody reads as a girls' thing or a boys' thing. The cheerleader and the jock sit on Spark's class, so they land on a robot.
- **No classroom teachers.** Matt: teachers "don't really fit the wasteland". People who teach out here do it as wasteland people, the way Gear Mira teaches as a mechanic.

### The player characters and their tropes

Matt asked for Australian wasteland names for the two Learners on [FAL-345](/FAL/issues/FAL-345) (2026-10-24). They're **Tilly Redback** (the female Learner) and **Banjo Brumby** (the male Learner). Tilly and Banjo come from the same bush song, "Waltzing Matilda", which Banjo Paterson wrote. A redback is a small, tough Australian spider, and a brumby is a wild outback horse. The names show on the look picker. Lines still call the player `{callsign}`, never Tilly or Banjo.

Each look starts in its own class, so each character has its own three tropes, one per branch. Any look can still take any class.

| Look | Default class | Branch: trope |
|---|---|---|
| Tilly Redback | Scout | Sprint: the late kid. Streak: the drama kid. Scavenge: the locker trader |
| Banjo Brumby | Ranger | Marksman: the yearbook photographer. Fortify: the drum major. Bounty Hunter: the hall monitor |
| Spark | Tinker | Repair: the cheerleader. Overclock: the jock. Analyze: the mathlete |

A trope shows in its branch's look nodes and as a subtitle on the Rewire bench, never in the branch's name or numbers. How each one shows is §5a of the [skill tree plan](/FAL/issues/FAL-345#document-plan).

## Psychological damage

This is Matt's answer to how talking does damage. In a talking encounter, a miss can be someone turning you down, laughing at your offer or yelling at you. **PSYCHOLOGICAL DAMAGE** then flashes up with the hit. The HP lost is the same as for any miss, so only the flavour changes. Traps, shoves, scalding coffee, an ambush and the Cell's hunger stay ordinary damage.

- Each encounter says which kind its misses do: `"damage": "psychological"` or `"physical"`. The format is in `writing/encounters/README.md`, and the spec is on [FAL-347](/FAL/issues/FAL-347).
- **Hit the character in the scene, never the student's maths.** Go after your haggling, your offer or your hat. A miss means a student just got a question wrong, so any line that sounds like "you're bad at this" is out, as the voice sheets already say.
- **It's a comic beat.** Keep the line short and let the flash land the joke. It shows after the answer, with the hit, never while a question is open.
