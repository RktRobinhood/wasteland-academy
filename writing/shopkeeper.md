# Bodge, the Bazaar's store owner

Matt asked for a crazy store owner in the spirit of Crazy Dave from Plants vs. Zombies (FAL-783, 2026-11-07). Bodge takes the spirit only: cheerful, unhinged, oddly generous, mostly nonsense with the odd bit of real advice, and on the player's side. Nothing of Dave's name, lines or look is used: no cookware on the head, no beard, no shouting a catchphrase. Her lines are in `game/data/shopkeeper.json` (written on FAL-789, read by the code on FAL-790).

A bodge is a rough repair that holds anyway, which suits someone who sells scrap.

## Who she is

Bodge runs the stall at the back of the Bazaar, out of a caravan whose wheels she sold years ago.
She'll buy anything and sell everything, and she slips something useless in with every sale, because she can't stand anyone leaving a shop empty-handed.
Most of what she says is nonsense, and now and then a line is the best advice in the wasteland. She can't tell which lines are which.

## Voice sheet

Same form as `writing/voice-sheets.md`. Bodge sits close to Dax, since both sell things, so the differences are spelled out at the end.

**Wants:** customers who come back off the road alive, so there's someone to talk to. Would never admit: the freebies are things she can't bear to throw away, and the caravan is very quiet at night.

**Vocabulary:** the junk drawer. Keys, buttons, bolts, tins, lamps, shelves, sand. Shop-patter words she half remembers ("sold!", "pleasure doing business", "no refunds"). Superstitions about luck and weather. No trade maths: no margin, per unit or cost, which are Dax's words.

**Rhythm:** short bursts that pile up, as if she's picking up a conversation she was already having with herself. Changes subject without warning. Asks questions and answers them herself, or doesn't bother. Exclamation marks on greetings, flat delivery on the strange parts.

**Tic and avoidance:** talks to her stock and her furniture as if they were staff. Gerald is the till, the lamp places bets, and the blue tin is in disgrace. She never says goodbye ("last time I said it, the roof left").

**Praise and correction** (Bodge doesn't teach, so here "right" is a sale and "not quite" is a sale that can't happen):

| A sale | No sale |
|---|---|
| "Sold! And a free key. Doesn't open anything I know of." | "Short on Scrap? Happens to the best of us. Happens to me hourly." |
| "Good choice. Great choice. Gerald, ring it up!" | "I'd give it to you, but Gerald's watching. He's very strict. He's a box." |
| "Yours now! Have this button too. It came off something important." | "Not enough for that. Go have a run and bring me whatever falls off." |
| "Have a bolt for luck. Talk to it if things get bad out there." | "You've got all of it. There isn't any more. I checked the back." |
| "No refunds, no returns. Regrets are allowed, but bring your own." | "That's as good as it gets. I tried making it better once. It caught fire." |

**Samples:**

- Cheerful, at the door: "Oh good, it's you. I was arguing with the shelf and losing."
- Nonsense that's secretly advice: "Stuck on something? Turn it upside down. Works on jars. Works on most things. Not soup."
- Warm, as the player leaves: "Go on, then. The wasteland's lucky to have you. Don't tell it I said so."

**Rules for her lines:** 90 characters at most. No prices or numbers, because the shop screen shows them. No `{callsign}`, since the Bazaar code doesn't fill it in. Nothing that only fits a body with feet or lungs, because the player can be SPARK. Two or three winks in the whole set at most: May as a storm, half a formula booklet kept like treasure, and her very long memoirs.

**Not the others:** Dax names people the player hasn't met and never says "free". Bodge names objects and says "free" all the time. Spark's jokes land on the maths and Bodge never mentions maths. Gear Mira finishes her sentences and Bodge abandons hers. Noma is slow and Bodge is quick. B0-LT glitches. Bodge works fine, she's just odd.

## Look brief for Artist (FAL-792)

Bodge is a short, wide woman in her fifties, built like a water barrel on thin legs, so her outline is a big round block with a small head on top and a long grabber sticking out to one side. Her one oddity is her goggles: the right lens is plain smoked glass, and the left is a thick brass jeweller's loupe that blows her left eye up to about three times the size of the right, so she always looks amazed. Her grey hair is shoved straight up by the goggle strap. She wears a patched rust-orange leather apron over a vest of hammered chrome bottle caps sewn on like fish scales, with fingerless gloves, a Bazaar-magenta scarf knotted at the throat, a ring of loose keys on her belt, and a single teal stripe of war paint across the bridge of her nose. Colours: rust orange and dark leather brown, chrome, magenta `#FF3DA7` on the scarf, and Scrap teal `#22D3C5` on the face stripe and the glint in the loupe. Her prop is a long, dented litter-picker grabber with a chrome claw, which she uses to pass things over the counter. In the idle frame she holds a small bolt up in the grabber's claw and peers at it through the loupe, and the big eye blinks once. Keep clear of Crazy Dave: nothing on her head but hair and goggles, no pan or pot anywhere, no beard, no stubble.
