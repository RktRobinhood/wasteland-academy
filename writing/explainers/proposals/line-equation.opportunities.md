## The opening call

The cold open asks a yes-or-no question ("Is that enough to pin down a whole line?"). That makes a weak call, since nearly everyone says yes. A number works better. Pin the aerial at (1, 4) with gradient 2: where does it cross the y-axis? The player types a number.

- 2: right.
- 4: read the pin's y. The recap says that's the pin, not the crossing.
- −1: that's where it crosses the x-axis, the third misconception.

## What the player plays with

**The mast and a pinned line (#221).** This lesson is the toy: a pin, a nut, and an aerial that swings.

Tasks: loosen the nut and swing the aerial through the pin. Lock it at gradient 2 and read where it crosses each axis. Move the pin to (3, −2), lock it at 4, and read c (−14). Find the pin and gradient that pass through A(1, 3) and B(4, 9).

A slip costs something small, from oupa's story: the nut comes loose, the aerial swings off, and it points at a cow two farms over.

## The worked examples

- **Watch:** example 1, (3, −2) with gradient 4. The why-tap on y − (−2) stays.
- **Build:** example 2, the line through A(1, 3) and B(4, 9):
  - line 1: `m = (9 − 3)/(4 − 1) = 2` / `m = (4 − 1)/(9 − 3) = 1/2` / `m = (9 − 1)/(4 − 3) = 8`
  - line 2: `y − 3 = 2(x − 1)` / `y − 1 = 2(x − 3)` / `y + 3 = 2(x + 1)` (the fill tap's choices)
  - line 3: `y = 2x + 1` / `y = 2x − 5` / `y = 2x − 1`
  - The check with B, the point not used, stays.
- **Exam layout:** example 3, P(−2, 7) and Q(4, −2), parts (a) to (c). (a) −3/2 becomes a typed answer, and so do the intercepts in (c).

## The long run of talk

It runs from the recall through the big idea, about 270 words. Rook pins the line, derives point-gradient form, rearranges it, defines both intercepts, and gives the third form. The last line runs on for about 45 words and ends "Sorry. Back on frequency."

There's a second long stretch: the end of example 3, the whole second-method section and the slip's set-up, about 180 words with no tap.

How to break it up:
- Swing and lock the aerial before Rook writes any form.
- The player reads both intercepts off the toy, then a tap on which one has x = 0.
- The second method (solve for c) could be a choice the player makes in example 3 part (b), "pin it, or solve for c", with the other way shown on the board only. That cuts most of the second-method talk and gets the script under 1,000.
