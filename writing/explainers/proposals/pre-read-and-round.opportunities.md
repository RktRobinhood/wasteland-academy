# Read the Gauge: opportunity pass

`ex.foundations.pre-read-and-round`, Tin-Can Tess. Notes for the rework (#223), not a rewrite.

Now: 7 player actions, a longest run of talk of about 260 words, and 1,010 words to record. The Foundations cap is 800.

## What must survive

- **Stories and tics.** No two gauges in the yard agree. The water tank "counts in whatever mood it's in that day", and the mood turns out to be a moth. Nan rounded everything, scones included. Tess's own log book, from before she knew better.
- **Tie-ins.** The recap sends the player to Mags, who wants every answer to three significant figures. Rook's `line-model` rounds 28.923… once, at the end, and says so.
- **Misconceptions.** Counting a step as one unit, mixing up significant figures with decimal places, rounding in two steps, and dropping a final zero.
- **Lines to keep word for word:**
  - "The boiler counts in fours, the fuel tank counts in two point fives, and the water tank counts in whatever mood it's in that day."
  - "My nan rounded everything. Her prices, her age, her recipes. Her scones were famous, for all the wrong reasons."
  - "Keep the zero on the end. One point five is only two significant figures. The zero says you measured that far."
  - "That water tank gauge, by the way? Fixed it. The mood turned out to be a moth."

## The opening call

The boiler gauge. Marks at 20 and 40, five steps between, and the needle three steps past 20. What does it read? The player types a number.

- 32: right.
- 23 (counted each step as one): the recap names the boiler, which counts in fours.

## What the player plays with

**The gauge with a needle (#221)**, for the first half. The player reads the marks, taps out the steps, and drags the needle to a reading.

Tasks: find what one step is worth on the boiler, the fuel tank and the water tank. Drag the needle to 17.5 litres.

**The second half has no toy.** Rounding to significant figures isn't a gauge. One idea would be a new readout toy: a GDC screen full of digits, where the player taps where to cut and the last digit rolls up or stays. See `opportunity-pass.md`. Without it, the rounding half is board and taps only.

## The worked examples

- **Watch:** example 1, the fuel gauge.
- **Build:** example 2, 0.0045678 m to 3 s.f.:
  - line 1: `first non-zero digit: the 4` / `the first 0` / `the 5`
  - line 2: `4, 5, 6, and the next digit is 7` / `0, 0, 4, and the next digit is 5` / `4, 5, 6, and the next digit is 8`
  - line 3: `0.00457` / `0.005` / `0.0046`
- **Exam layout:** example 3, the cylinder tank. Parts (a), 1.3 m, and (b), 3.31 m³, become typed answers. The why-tap on rounding once, at the end, stays.

## The long run of talk

It runs from the recall through the big idea, about 260 words. It is two lessons in one run: the scale (about 90 words) and then rounding (about 150). The gauge breaks up the first half. The second half needs the readout toy, or two taps at least: count the significant figures in 1.4972, and pick 1.50 or 1.5.

Of the 11, this script most needs a look at whether it is one lesson or two. It also has the most misconceptions (four).

Nothing is missing. The gauges, the sheet of tin and the tanks are all in the yard.
