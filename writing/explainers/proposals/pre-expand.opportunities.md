# Fill the Rectangle: opportunity pass

`ex.foundations.pre-expand`, Tin-Can Tess. Notes for the rework (#223), not a rewrite.

Now: 7 player actions, a longest run of talk of about 230 words, and 945 words to record. The Foundations cap is 800.

## What must survive

- **Stories and tics.** The hole in the shed and the ute next to it with a dent the same shape. The roof patched without measuring. The x = 1 check, used five times, which is the habit the lesson teaches. The stripped-bolt tin, which `pre-balance` also uses (see `opportunity-pass.md`).
- **Tie-ins.** The recall is 3(x + 4). The recap points to factorising ("you get the area, and you find the sides"). The y-intercept part of example 3 is the same idea as `pre-balance` example 3 and Rook's line lessons.
- **Misconceptions.** (x + r)² = x² + r², the wrong sign on a product, and multiplying d by k in k(x + r)² + d.
- **Lines to keep word for word:**
  - "No idea who. The ute parked next to it has a dent the exact same shape, mind."
  - "I patched a roof once without measuring. Lovely job. Covered half the roof and most of the neighbour's."
  - "Those two middle strips are the bit everyone leaves out."
  - "And the ute's owner has offered to pay for the tin. Funny, that."

## The opening call

At the moment the cold open asks for an area with letters in it, which nobody can call. Give the shed a size instead: x is 10, so the hole is 13 by 12. How much tin is that? The player types a number.

- 156: right, and later x² + 5x + 6 at x = 10 gives 156 too, which is the check the lesson teaches.
- 106 (x² + 6, squaring the x and multiplying the numbers): the two strips are missing. The recap can say so.

## What the player plays with

**The tin sheet (#221): cut a rectangle into parts.** The player drags a cut along each side where the plus is, and the four pieces show their areas.

Tasks: cut the (x + 3) by (x + 2) sheet into four and label each piece. Slide x and watch the pieces change size, while the corner piece stays at 6. Cut the square patch (x + 4)² and find the two strips.

A slip costs something small: the patch comes out short, rain drips through the gap, and Tess gets the bucket.

## The worked examples

- **Watch:** example 1, (x + 4)(x + 5).
- **Build:** example 2, (x + 4)²:
  - line 1: `(x + 4)(x + 4)` / `x² + 4²` / `x² + 16`
  - line 2: `= x² + 4x + 4x + 16` / `= x² + 4x + 16` / `= x² + 16`
  - line 3: `= x² + 8x + 16` / `= x² + 16x + 16` / `= x² + 4x + 16`
- **Exam layout:** example 3, 2(x − 3)² + 1, show that, then the y-intercept. In part (a) the line `2x² − 12x + 18 + 1` could be built from tiles, with `2x² − 12x + 18 + 2` (the third misconception) as a wrong one. Part (b), (0, 19), becomes a typed answer.

## The long run of talk

It runs from the recall through the big idea, about 230 words. Tess cuts the sheet, names each piece, adds them, defines like terms, and does the x = 1 check, all herself. The cuts and labels go to the player. The x = 1 check could be a typed answer ("put x = 1 into both: what do you get?").
