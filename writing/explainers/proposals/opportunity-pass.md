How the numbers were counted: "Actions" is every tap and turn on the main path. "Longest run" is spoken words on the main path between two actions, not counting the branch line the player hears, so it reads about 20 lower than the exemplar's way of counting. ## What came up across the 11

**Toys.** Three toys cover eight of the 11. The mast and pinned line serves Rook's three line lessons. The relay serves `inverse-undo` (run backwards) and `pre-substitute` (as Tess's counting drum). Seat rows and the ledger column serve both of Mags's. Each Foundations toy on the #221 list fits exactly one script: scales, tin sheet, gauge, price lever.

**No good toy.** The rounding half of `pre-read-and-round`. The gauge covers reading a scale, and nothing on the list covers significant figures. Either the catalogue gets a readout toy (a GDC screen where the player taps where to cut and the last digit rolls up or stays), or the script is split into two lessons. It's the only script with two ideas in its one long run.

**Every script is over its cap.** All five Foundations scripts are over 800 (894 to 1,010), and all six others are over 1,000 (1,042 to 1,401). "Recorded words must not go up" (#223) is the easy rule. The cap is the one that forces cuts. The places to cut are the recall's not-watched version (43 to 73 words in each of the six scripts that have one), the second-method sections in `arithmetic-series` and `line-equation`, and talk the toy now does.

## For the format (#216)

- **A tapped call, not only a typed or dialled one.** `gradient`'s question (which track is gentler?) is the best cold open of the 11, and it's a choice between two things, not a number. `pre-substitute`'s call has two right answers (9 and −9, for two different questions). The recap rule in #216 assumes one matching value.
- **The recall section has no place in the four acts.** Six of the 11 have a watched and a not-watched recall, and the other five have a single tap with no callback line. Mags's recall in `arithmetic-sequence` ("Hang on to that shape. It's back in a minute.") sets up the lesson. Most of the others could become the first toy task or a line in act 1. The exemplar leaves this open too.
- **Each opening call should come with its misconception values.** Every note lists them: 182 and 160 for row 40, 37 and 5.5 for the tower's 17, 80, 83 and 30 for the airtime. Their recap lines are where the slips get named.
- **Settings in examples 1 and 2.** #216 checks the first worked example for a setting. The bare ones are in `pre-substitute`, `pre-balance`, `pre-expand`, `gradient`, `inverse-undo` and `line-equation`. Rook's line lessons are the barest. Mags's and `line-model` are fully set.

## For the toy catalogue (#221)

- **The relay needs two options:** a reverse switch (`inverse-undo`, already planned) and a brackets clip (`pre-substitute`), so the same input goes in with or without brackets. With the clip, Tess's drum doesn't need its own toy.
- **The scales need crates** (a bag with bolts taken out) for `pre-balance`'s bracket slip, 4(x − 2).
- **The ledger column needs a flip** (a reversed copy laid beside it) for `arithmetic-series`. Gauss's trick then becomes something the player does.
- **The line toy needs labelled axes and a domain** for `line-model`: cost against minutes, fuel against hours, and an end where the fuel runs out.
- **A readout toy for rounding**, or a reason the lesson has none (see above).

## Repeats across scripts

The format 2 checker already looks for repeated jokes. These show up across lessons:

- Tess's tin of stripped bolts closes the spot-the-slip in both `pre-balance` and `pre-expand`. Keep it in one.
- Rook's "I wrote that wrong line myself" closes the spot-the-slip in `inverse-undo` and in the exemplar. Keep it in one.
- Rook's oupa has a story in all five Rook scripts (radio, bakkie, socks and boots, aerial, generator). That's his tic, and it should stay, but no two should land in the same act.
- "Sorry. Back on frequency." closes a run-on line in `gradient`, `inverse-undo` and `line-equation`. It's always the longest line in the big idea. The rework should keep the tic and shorten the line.
