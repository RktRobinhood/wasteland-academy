# Relic lessons: the design-down lesson map

Generated by `node tools/lessons.mjs --write` from `lessons.json`. Edit the JSON, not this file.

A Relic lesson is a 3 to 6 minute playable episode (GitHub issue #13): a character shows a broken machine, the player takes the controls, the character reacts to what the player actually did, the recipe engine sets an independent task, and a short debrief turns the machine into notation. **Each lesson teaches one idea, not one question type.** This map works back from the exam question types in `question-types.json` to the ideas and prior learning behind them (FAL-327).

#### Fill the Rectangle (`lesson.fn.pre-expand`)

- **Objective:** Expand brackets by multiplying every term in one bracket by every term in the other.
- **Idea:** (x + r)(x + s) is the area of a rectangle with sides x + r and x + s, so it splits into the four pieces x², rx, sx and rs, and (x + r)² has two middle strips, not none.
- **Scenario (building or balancing expressions):** B0-LT is tiling a workshop floor with an x by x slab, x by 1 strips and 1 by 1 tiles. The player drags tiles to fill a rectangle with the given side lengths and reads off its total area; B0-LT reacts when the player fills a square of side x + 4 with only x² and 16. The debrief writes the area as the expanded quadratic.
- **Apply:** `fn-pre-expand`: double-bracket, perfect-square, vertex-form-expand.
- **Skills:** prior learning (level 0).
- **Misconceptions the react stage targets:** `fn-pre-expand#0`, `fn-pre-expand#1`, `fn-pre-expand#2`, `fn-pre-expand#3`.
- **Alignment:** IB SL2.6 Quadratic functions. GCSE Algebra 4.

#### Find the Sides (`lesson.fn.pre-factorise`)

- **Objective:** Factorise a quadratic expression into brackets.
- **Idea:** Factorising runs expanding backwards: arrange the area x² + bx + c into a rectangle, and its sides are the factors, built from two numbers that multiply to c and add to b.
- **Scenario (building or balancing expressions):** B0-LT has a pile of floor tiles and needs to know the sides of the rectangle they make. The player arranges the slab, the strips and the unit tiles into a single rectangle and reads off its sides; B0-LT reacts when the signs are swapped or the player tries to make a rectangle from x² + 9. The debrief writes the sides as the factorised form.
- **Apply:** `fn-pre-factorise`: common-factor, monic-trinomial, difference-of-squares.
- **Skills:** prior learning (level 0).
- **Misconceptions the react stage targets:** `fn-pre-factorise#0`, `fn-pre-factorise#1`, `fn-pre-factorise#2`, `fn-pre-factorise#3`.
- **Alignment:** IB SL2.6 Quadratic functions; SL2.7 Solving quadratic equations and inequalities; the discriminant. Common Core HSA.SSE.A.2. GCSE Algebra 4.

#### Either Valve Shuts It (`lesson.fn.zero-product`)

- **Objective:** Solve a quadratic in factorised form by setting each factor equal to zero.
- **Idea:** A product is zero exactly when one of its factors is zero, so a(x − p)(x − q) = 0 when x = p or x = q; dividing both sides by x can throw away the root x = 0.
- **Scenario (sliders and dials):** B0-LT's coolant pipe has two valves in a row, and the flow is the product of their openings, (x − 3)(x + 2). The player turns the main dial to find every setting where the flow stops; B0-LT reacts when the player looks for the stop at x = −3 or forgets the second valve. The debrief writes the two settings as the roots x = 3 and x = −2.
- **Apply:** `fn-solve-quadratic`: solve-factorisable, solve-no-constant.
- **Skills:** `SL2.7.solve-equation`, `SL2.6.factorised-form`, `SL2.4.zeros-intercepts`.
- **Misconceptions the react stage targets:** `SL2.6.factorised-form#0`, `SL2.7.solve-equation#0`, `SL2.7.solve-equation#1`, `SL2.4.zeros-intercepts#1`, `SL2.4.zeros-intercepts#2`.
- **Alignment:** IB SL2.6 Quadratic functions; SL2.7 Solving quadratic equations and inequalities; the discriminant. Common Core HSA.SSE.B.3.a, HSA.REI.B.4.b. GCSE Algebra 18. A-level B3.
- **Materials:** [Illustrative Mathematics Algebra 1, Unit 7: Quadratic equations](https://im.kendallhunt.com/HS/teachers/1/7/index.html) (CC BY 4.0).

#### The Fountain's Mirror (`lesson.fn.parabola-symmetry`)

- **Objective:** Use the symmetry of a parabola to find its axis, its vertex and a missing root.
- **Idea:** A parabola is symmetric about the vertical line through its vertex, so points at the same height, such as the two roots, sit the same distance either side, and the axis is halfway between them.
- **Scenario (placing geometric objects):** B0-LT's fountain throws an arc of water that should land in two buckets the same distance from a central post, but the post is in the wrong place. The player places the post, and the arc's mirror image swings across it until the two halves match; B0-LT reacts when the post goes at the y-intercept or the axis is written as y = h. The debrief writes the axis as x = (p + q)/2, and as x = −b/(2a) for the general form.
- **Apply:** `fn-quadratic-features`: axis-from-roots, second-root-from-axis, vertex-from-factorised, axis-from-equal-outputs.
- **Skills:** `SL2.6.axis-symmetry`, `SL2.4.symmetry`, `SL2.6.vertex`.
- **Misconceptions the react stage targets:** `SL2.6.axis-symmetry#0`, `SL2.6.axis-symmetry#1`, `SL2.4.symmetry#0`, `SL2.4.symmetry#1`, `SL2.4.symmetry#2`.

#### The Crane Hook (`lesson.fn.vertex-form`)

- **Objective:** Read the vertex, the axis and whether the vertex is a maximum or a minimum from y = a(x − h)² + k.
- **Idea:** y = a(x − h)² + k is y = ax² translated by the vector (h, k), so its vertex is (h, k), its axis is x = h, and the sign of a decides whether the vertex is a minimum or a maximum.
- **Scenario (graph manipulation):** B0-LT's crane swings its hook along a parabola, and the lowest point keeps missing the loading bay. The player drags the turning point onto the bay and pulls a shape handle to widen, narrow or flip the arc, while the equation in vertex form updates; B0-LT reacts when the player reads (x + 3) as a move to the right or calls a highest point a minimum. The debrief links the drag to the conveyor's translation vector (h, k).
- **Apply:** `fn-quadratic-features`: vertex-from-vertex-form, h-k-from-vertex, max-or-min, y-intercept-from-vertex-form.
- **Skills:** `SL2.6.vertex`, `SL2.6.sketch`.
- **Misconceptions the react stage targets:** `SL2.6.vertex#0`, `SL2.6.vertex#1`, `SL2.6.vertex#2`, `SL2.6.axis-symmetry#2`, `SL2.6.sketch#0`.

#### The Missing Corner (`lesson.fn.complete-square`)

- **Objective:** Complete the square to write a quadratic in vertex form.
- **Idea:** x² + bx is a square of side x + b/2 with a corner of (b/2)² missing, so x² + bx + c = (x + b/2)² − (b/2)² + c; when there is a leading coefficient a, take a out first and multiply the correction by a.
- **Scenario (building or balancing expressions):** B0-LT is patching a square hatch from scrap tiles: one big square, some long strips and some unit tiles. The player arranges the tiles into a square, finds the corner that is missing, and balances it by borrowing from the unit tiles; B0-LT reacts when the player forgets to take the borrowed corner away again or lays the strips with the wrong sign. The debrief writes the finished hatch as (x + b/2)² − (b/2)² + c.
- **Apply:** `fn-quadratic-features`: vertex-form-from-general, vertex-from-general.
- **Skills:** `SL2.6.complete-square`.
- **Misconceptions the react stage targets:** `SL2.6.complete-square#0`, `SL2.6.complete-square#1`, `SL2.6.complete-square#2`.
- **Alignment:** IB SL2.6 Quadratic functions. Common Core HSA.SSE.B.3.b, HSA.REI.B.4.a. GCSE Algebra 11. A-level B3.
- **Materials:** [Illustrative Mathematics Algebra 1, Unit 7: Quadratic equations](https://im.kendallhunt.com/HS/teachers/1/7/index.html) (CC BY 4.0).

#### One More Bolt (`lesson.fn.quadratic-from-clues`)

- **Objective:** Find the equation of a quadratic from its roots or its vertex and one more point.
- **Idea:** Choose the form that holds what you know, the roots in a(x − p)(x − q) or the vertex in a(x − h)² + k; every value of a fits those clues, and one more point picks out the only a that works.

The player picks the form that uses the hooks, then turns a dial for a and watches a family of cables sag deeper or shallower until one passes the bolt; B0-LT reacts when the player assumes a = 1 or writes (x − 1) for a hook at −1. The debrief substitutes the bolt's coordinates to find a in one line.
- **Apply:** `fn-quadratic-from-conditions`: a-from-roots-and-point, a-from-vertex-and-point, coefficient-from-point.
- **Skills:** `SL2.6.find-equation`, `SL2.6.factorised-form`.

#### Roots That Split (`lesson.fn.pre-surds`)

- **Objective:** Simplify a surd, and the fraction that an exact quadratic answer comes in.
- **Idea:** √(ab) = √a × √b, so √48 = √16 × √3 = 4√3; but the root of a sum does not split, and in a fraction such as (4 + √12)/2 every term on top is divided.
- **Scenario (sorting and matching):** B0-LT's sorting chute takes crates labelled with roots like √48 and has to send each one out in its simplest form. The player sorts crates into the bins for their simplest forms, pulling out the biggest square factor first; B0-LT reacts when the player splits √(9 + 16) into √9 + √16 or divides only one term of a fraction. The debrief writes each sorted crate as a√b.
- **Apply:** `fn-pre-surds`: simplify-root, formula-fraction.
- **Skills:** prior learning (level 0).
- **Misconceptions the react stage targets:** `fn-pre-surds#0`, `fn-pre-surds#1`, `fn-pre-surds#2`.
- **Alignment:** IB SL2.7 Solving quadratic equations and inequalities; the discriminant. Common Core HSN.RN.A.2. GCSE Number 8. A-level B2.

#### Does It Reach the Floor? (`lesson.fn.discriminant`)

- **Objective:** Use the discriminant to say whether a quadratic has two, one or no real roots.
- **Idea:** Δ = b² − 4ac is the part of the quadratic formula under the square root: Δ > 0 gives two real roots, Δ = 0 gives one repeated root where the vertex touches the x-axis, and Δ < 0 gives none.
- **Scenario (predict, then simulate):** B0-LT's ball launcher fires arcs over a trench, and the robot wants to know before each shot whether it will cross the trench floor twice, skim it or clear it. The player predicts the outcome from the shot's a, b and c, then fires; B0-LT reacts when the player says a negative Δ means two negative roots or slips on −4ac when c is negative. The debrief writes the three cases as Δ > 0, Δ = 0 and Δ < 0.
- **Apply:** `fn-discriminant`: nature-from-equation, delta-sign-from-graph, graph-from-delta.
- **Skills:** `SL2.7.discriminant`, `SL2.7.solve-equation`.
- **Misconceptions the react stage targets:** `SL2.7.discriminant#0`, `SL2.7.discriminant#1`, `SL2.7.discriminant#2`.
- **Alignment:** IB SL2.7 Solving quadratic equations and inequalities; the discriminant. Common Core HSA.REI.B.4.b. A-level B3.

#### Above the Line (`lesson.fn.graph-inequality`)

#### One In, One Out (`lesson.fn.function-machine`)

- **Objective:** Use f(x) notation to find the output for an input, and the input for an output.
- **Idea:** f(a) is the output when a goes in, while f(x) = k runs the other way and asks which inputs give k; so the y-intercept is f(0) and the zeros are the solutions of f(x) = 0.
- **Scenario (drag and position):** B0-LT's stamping machine takes a numbered crate and prints a new number on it, and the order sheet mixes up what comes out of 3 with what has to go in to get 3. #### One Button, One Token (`lesson.fn.is-function`)

- **Objective:** Decide whether a rule, table or graph is a function, and whether it is one-to-one.
- **Idea:** A function gives each input exactly one output, so a vertical line meets its graph at most once; a function can be undone only if each output also comes from exactly one input, which a horizontal line tests.
- **Scenario (sorting and matching):** B0-LT's token dispenser jams whenever one button gives out two different tokens. #### What Goes In, What Comes Out (`lesson.fn.domain-range`)

- **Objective:** Find the inputs a function accepts and the outputs it can give.
- **Idea:** The domain is every input the rule and the context allow (no square root of a negative, no log of zero or less, no division by zero); the range is every output those inputs make, fixed by a vertex, an asymptote or the endpoints.
- **Scenario (sliders and dials):** B0-LT's filter machine rejects some inputs with a clank and only ever pumps out certain readings. The player sweeps the input dial along the number line, marks where the machine clanks, and watches each output land on a tally board beside the y-axis; B0-LT reacts when the player stops at the endpoints and misses a peak, or counts an asymptote value as an output. The debrief writes the clank-free inputs as the domain, in terms of x, and the tally board as the range, in terms of y.
- **Apply:** `fn-domain-range`: domain-root, domain-log-or-fraction, range-quadratic, range-rational.
- **Skills:** `SL2.2.domain`, `SL2.2.range`.

#### The Mirror Line (`lesson.fn.inverse-reflection`)

- **Objective:** Draw the graph of an inverse by reflecting in y = x, and use that symmetry to find its points and asymptotes.
- **Idea:** Undoing f swaps every input-output pair, so (a, b) on f becomes (b, a) on f⁻¹, and the graph of f⁻¹ is the mirror image of the graph of f in the line y = x.
- **Scenario (placing geometric objects):** B0-LT finds a cracked holo-projector that should show a machine's undo track, but only half of the picture works. The player places a mirror strip on the screen and turns it until the marked bolts on f land on their swapped partners, and the undo track appears; B0-LT reacts when the player mirrors in an axis instead of y = x. The debrief writes the swap (a, b) → (b, a) and notes that a graph symmetric in y = x is its own inverse.
- **Apply:** `fn-inverse-reflection`: image-point, sketch-inverse, intercepts-of-inverse, is-self-inverse.
- **Skills:** `SL2.2.inverse-undo`, `SL2.4.symmetry`, `SL2.5.identity`. Assumes from other islands: `SL5.11.area-between-curves`.

#### Rise per Step (`lesson.fn.gradient`)

- **Objective:** Find a gradient as the change in y for each step of 1 in x.
- **Idea:** A straight line has the same gradient everywhere: the rise divided by the run between any two of its points, taken in the same order, and its sign says whether the line goes up or down.
- **Scenario (sliders and dials):** B0-LT is building a ramp for the delivery bot out of identical steps, and the bot slips wherever the steps change. The player sets the rise of one step with a dial, then picks any two bolts on the ramp and checks that rise over run gives the same number; B0-LT reacts when the player divides the run by the rise or subtracts the coordinates in different orders. The debrief writes the gradient as m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁).
- **Apply:** `fn-line-from-conditions`: gradient-from-two-points, gradient-from-graph, gradient-from-general.
- **Skills:** `SL2.1.gradient`.

#### Keep the Scales Level (`lesson.fn.pre-balance`)

- **Objective:** Solve a linear equation by doing the same thing to both sides.
- **Idea:** An equation is a balance: adding, subtracting, multiplying or dividing both sides by the same thing keeps it level, so each step can undo one operation until x stands alone.
- **Scenario (building or balancing expressions):** B0-LT's weighing scale holds bags with an unknown number of bolts and some loose bolts on both pans. The player takes the same load off both pans, or splits both pans the same way, one move at a time, until a single bag balances a pile of bolts; B0-LT reacts when a move is made to one pan only or a bracket is multiplied into its first term only. The debrief writes each move as a line of algebra.
- **Apply:** `fn-pre-linear-equations`: two-step, both-sides, brackets.

#### Pin and Swing (`lesson.fn.line-equation`)

- **Objective:** Write the equation of a line from a point and a gradient, or from two points.
- **Idea:** A point and a gradient fix exactly one line: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) is the line y = mx moved to pass through (x₁, y₁), and every point on the line satisfies its equation.
- **Scenario (drag and position):** B0-LT needs a straight guide wire through one fixed bolt at a set slope. The player pins the wire to the bolt and swings it to the slope, then checks other bolts against its equation; B0-LT reacts when the player writes x + x₁ and the wire jumps to the mirror-image bolt, or gives the y-intercept as (c, 0). The debrief writes the wire as y − y₁ = m(x − x₁), then as y = mx + c and as ax + by + d = 0.
- **Apply:** `fn-line-from-conditions`: equation-point-gradient, equation-two-points, unknown-coordinate, general-form.
- **Skills:** `SL2.1.equation-of-line`, `SL2.1.intercepts`. Assumes from other islands: `SL3.1.midpoint-3d`.

#### The Quarter Turn (`lesson.fn.perpendicular`)

- **Objective:** Find the gradient of a parallel or a perpendicular line, and write its equation.
- **Idea:** Parallel lines take the same step, so they share a gradient; turning a step of (1, m) through a quarter-turn gives (−m, 1), so a perpendicular line has gradient −1/m and the two gradients multiply to −1.
- **Scenario (placing geometric objects):** B0-LT needs a brace bolted at right angles to a sloping beam. The player places a step triangle on the beam, turns it through a quarter-turn, and reads the new rise and run off the grid; B0-LT reacts when the player only changes the sign, or only flips the fraction. The debrief writes the turned step as the gradient −1/m and checks that m × (−1/m) = −1.
- **Apply:** `fn-line-from-conditions`: perpendicular-gradient, parallel-through-point, perpendicular-through-point.
- **Skills:** `SL2.1.parallel-perpendicular`.

#### Start Plus Rate (`lesson.fn.line-model`)

- **Objective:** Build a straight-line model and say what its gradient and intercept mean in context.
- **Idea:** A quantity that changes by the same amount for each unit of input is linear: the intercept is the starting amount, the gradient is the rate per unit, and the model only makes sense on a sensible domain.

#### Two Lamps, One Point (`lesson.fn.pre-simultaneous`)

- **Objective:** Solve two linear equations in two unknowns at the same time.

#### The Broken Conveyor (`lesson.fn.translation`)

- **Objective:** Move a graph left, right, up or down, and write the function for the moved graph.
- **Idea:** Replacing x with x − a moves every point of a graph a units to the right, and adding b to the output moves it b units up, so y = f(x − a) + b is the graph of f translated by the vector (a, b).
- **Scenario (drag and position):** B0-LT's conveyor rail is bent out of line, so the delivery bot keeps dropping crates beside the bin. The player drags the rail sideways and then up, and the rail's equation updates as it moves; B0-LT reacts when the rail goes the wrong way or overshoots, and the crates land when the rail matches the target. The debrief shows that moving the rail 3 to the right turned f(x) into f(x − 3), and that y = f(x − a) + b is a move by the vector (a, b).

#### The Spring Press (`lesson.fn.stretch`)

- **Scenario (sliders and dials):** B0-LT's spring press stamps out dish panels that come out too tall or too narrow. The player turns one dial that scales the output and one that scales the input, and watches the panel's outline stretch; B0-LT points out that setting the input dial to 2 made the panel half as wide, not twice as wide. #### Moves in Order (`lesson.fn.transform-order`)

## The other islands (big ideas only)

Their catalogues are at summary level, so these are estimates, not lesson entries. Each will be designed down the same way once its catalogue is filled in.

### The Scrap Fields (Number and algebra) (topic 1, traders-caravan)

About 20 to 26 lessons. 16 exam types and 15 extension types. Most prior learning is shared with Functions.

- Scale by powers of ten: standard form keeps very large and very small numbers comparable.
- Add the same each step: arithmetic sequences and series (a sequence is a line sampled at whole numbers).
- Multiply the same each step: geometric sequences, compound interest and depreciation.
- Add up a pattern: sum formulas, sigma notation, and why a sum to infinity exists only when |r| < 1.
- Powers follow laws: integer, negative and rational exponents.
- A logarithm is a power: log laws, change of base and solving exponential equations.
- True for every x: identities, proof, and finding unknown constants by comparing coefficients.
- The nCr pattern: binomial expansion and its coefficients.

### The Dry Flats (Geometry and trigonometry) (topic 3, broken-observatory)

About 24 to 30 lessons. 21 exam types and 2 extension types.

- Measure space: distance, midpoint, volume and surface area in 3D.
- An angle fixes a ratio: right-angled trigonometry from similar triangles.
- Any triangle: the sine rule, the cosine rule, the ambiguous case and the area ½ab sin C.
- Directions as angles: bearings, elevation and depression.
- Radians measure angle by arc length: arcs, sectors and segments.
- The unit circle: exact values, signs in each quadrant and related angles.
- Identities rewrite trigonometric expressions: Pythagorean and double-angle identities.
- Waves are transformed sine curves: amplitude, period and principal axis.
- A trigonometric equation has many solutions: find all of them in an interval.
- Periodic models: use and fit a·sin(b(x − c)) + d in context.

### Radio Spires (Statistics and probability) (topic 4, signal-bunker)

About 24 to 30 lessons. 24 exam types and 8 extension types.

- Who you ask matters: sampling, bias and types of data.
- Centre and spread: summary statistics, and what adding to or scaling every value does to them.
- Pictures of data: cumulative frequency, box plots, histograms and outliers.
- Lines through data: correlation, regression, and when a prediction can be trusted.
- Probability as long-run frequency: sample spaces, complements and expected counts.
- Combining events: Venn diagrams, tree diagrams, the addition rule, conditional probability and independence.
- Random variables: distributions that add up to 1, and expected value as a long-run average.
- Counting successes: the binomial distribution.
- The bell curve: normal probabilities, symmetry, standardising and the inverse normal.

### The Engine Core (Calculus) (topic 5, engine-graveyard)

About 24 to 30 lessons. 18 exam types and 13 extension types. Differential calculus would sit in the Engine Graveyard and integral calculus in the Flooded Reservoir.

- The derivative is a rate: from chord gradients to the gradient of the tangent.
- Rules for rates: the power, chain, product and quotient rules.
- Tangents and normals: the lines that meet a curve at a point at a known angle.
- Where a graph rises, falls and turns: increasing and decreasing, stationary points and concavity.
- The best value: optimisation from a constraint.
- Motion: displacement, velocity and acceleration as one chain of rates.
- Undo the derivative: antiderivatives, + C and boundary conditions.
- Add up many small pieces: the definite integral as area, and the area between two curves.
- Distance from velocity: integrate speed, not velocity, to find the total distance.
