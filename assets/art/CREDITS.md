## Licence: ChatGPT images in a paid release (#199, #201)

Checked 2026-11-16 by the Artist (FAL-1034).

- **Ownership.** OpenAI's Terms of Use, section "Content", say that as between the user and OpenAI, the user
  owns the output: "We hereby assign to you all our right, title, and interest, if any, in and to Output."
  The terms allow output to be used for any lawful purpose, including selling it, as long as the user follows
  the terms and the usage policies. Matt generates the images on his own account, so he owns them, and the
  game may ship them.
- **Copyright protection is thin.** Ownership under a contract is not the same as copyright. In the US, purely
  machine-made images get no copyright protection without human authorship. So a competitor could copy the
  map art, but that doesn't stop us shipping or selling it. The game's code, text, maths content and voices
  are protected separately.
- **Not unique.** The terms say that other users may get similar output. Our pictures are built around our own
  places, so this is unlikely to matter.
- **Stores.** Steam asks developers to disclose AI-generated content that players see, hear or read. Since
  January 2026 that is the form for pre-generated content, which must follow the same rules as any other
  content. The art here counts as pre-generated, so the Steam page will need that disclosure. No rule found
  for Google Play or the App Store forbids generated art.
- **No other game's name in the prompts.** The cards describe the look in our own words, and the world map is
  attached as the style reference. That keeps another game's trade dress out of the record.
- **How sure.** openai.com refused the page to an automated fetch (HTTP 403). The quotes above come from
  secondary sources that agree with each other (terms.law, conductatlas.com). One microstock blog claims that
  free ChatGPT accounts don't get commercial rights. Nothing else backs that claim, and OpenAI's consumer
  terms apply to every plan. Before the first paid store submission, someone should read the current terms
  on openai.com once and note the date read here.

**Verdict:** nothing found blocks a paid release.
